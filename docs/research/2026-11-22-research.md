## In plain words

1. **The curriculum** (ePerusteet, peruste 6828810, oppiaine FI 6832794) [V]:

   | Module | Name | Status | Objectives / contents |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | FI1 | Johdatus filosofiseen ajatteluun | compulsory, 2 op | 5 / 5 |
   | FI2 | Etiikka | compulsory, 2 op | 6 / 6 |
   | FI3 | Yhteiskuntafilosofia | national optional, 2 op | 5 / 6 |
   | FI4 | Totuus | national optional, 2 op | 6 / 7 |

   FI1 is about what philosophy is, argument and valid reasoning, concepts and thought experiments, four basic
   distinctions (mind and matter, freedom and necessity, conceptual and empirical, objective and subjective), and
   knowledge: information versus knowledge, everyday versus scientific, science versus pseudoscience, and how claims
   are tested in other lukio subjects. Ethics is FI2, not FI1. The curriculum names no philosophers.
2. **Primary texts are public domain and online in the original** [V for the ones opened]: Plato and Aristotle in
   Greek on Perseus (Burnet, Ross), Descartes in Latin on Wikisource (Adam–Tannery), Epictetus and Epicurus in Greek on
   Wikisource, Hume's 1748 *Enquiry* in English, Kant's 1784 *Aufklärung* essay in German. Perseus opens some texts in
   a Loeb English translation by default; those are not copied, only the Greek. Project Gutenberg was down (503)
   throughout, so its links wait for a check.
3. **Translations:** the site translates short passages itself, labelled "suomennos: sivusto", and a second agent
   checks each against the original. Older Finnish translations exist (Plato's *Gorgias* 1899, *Symposion* 1919), but
   no translator's death year was read, so none is used yet [U]. The quotation right (tekijänoikeuslaki 22 §) allows
   short quotes from copyrighted works for a purpose such as teaching; modern translations are only named [V].
4. **Materials** are thin and mostly link-out: OPH's four LOPS support pages for filosofia, Ensyklopedia Logos, Yle
   Abitreenit, Stanford and Internet Encyclopedias, Wi-Phi and Crash Course (English). Tieteen termipankki is the only
   open item (CC BY-SA, linked only). No CC course shells were found.
5. **The exam** [V on the spring 2025 grading page]: nine tasks, 120 points. Part 1 has six 20-point tasks (answer
   three to five), Part 2 three 30-point tasks (answer up to two). Formats: concept essays, material essays (a quote
   or a case), 20-item multiple choice, definitions with examples (4 points, 600 characters), a method task, and
   30-point tasks in parts. Grading weighs relevance (osuvuus) most, then coherence and persuasiveness. All four
   modules appear in every sitting. Terveystieto's A/B/C parts and its 5-point deduction do not carry over.
6. **Images:** nano-banana-2.1 costs $0.0336 and has been used on the opinto-ohjaus site. FI1's plan is 12 calm
   scenes (the cave, the ship of Theseus, a forked path, an arch of premises) for about $0.40, $0.60 with redos;
   9 Mermaid diagrams (the branches of philosophy, argument structure, validity, necessary and sufficient conditions,
   mind and body views, freedom views, Hume's fork, knowledge, testing claims in four subjects); and 5 public-domain
   artworks from Wikimedia Commons (Raphael's Plato and Aristotle, David's *Death of Socrates*, portraits of Descartes
   and Hume, Saenredam's cave). The whole site would cost about $2.5–3 in images; about $9 of the grant is left.
7. **The base:** the terveystieto site's engine formats, lesson plans, rubric section and workflows carry over. New:
   a primary-text box (original and translation, as blockquotes, because the engine renders no boxes), worldview
   neutrality, and two new checkers (sources and translations; worldview and neutrality) in place of the health ones.

## Open questions and defaults

- **Photographs of public-domain paintings:** the EU copyright directive (article 14) says faithful reproductions of
  public-domain visual art are not protected, and Finland has implemented it [L]. Default: use Commons PD-Art files
  with their source and credit. The operator can override.
- **Unverified links** (Gutenberg, some Perseus Greek pages, Hume's section VIII, Rousseau) are checked before use.
- **Engine gaps** for this site: no box styling, the page language is fixed to Finnish, the date scanner flags
  "1.7"-style citations, and the link pattern breaks on URLs with parentheses (percent-encode them). They go into
  `docs/engine-issues.md`.
- **Reviewers:** a filosofia teacher, and readers of Greek, Latin and German for the translations.
