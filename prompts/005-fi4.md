## Expected answer, written before the work [M]

1. **Ten topics** (order 31–40): opinions and factual claims; language, meaning and reference (Frege); truth theories
   (correspondence, coherence, pragmatism); necessary and contingent (Leibniz, Kant's analytic and synthetic);
   realism and idealism (Berkeley); consciousness and the mind–body problem revisited; sources of knowledge
   (rationalism, empiricism, Kant); scepticism and its limits; scientific reasoning (Bacon, induction, falsification
   paraphrased) and the ethics of science; explanation and understanding (Dilthey, von Wright paraphrased).
2. **Primary texts** in German (Frege, Wittgenstein, Kant, Dilthey), English (Berkeley, Locke, James, Peirce), French
   (Leibniz, Descartes), Latin (Bacon), Greek (Plato's *Theaetetus* or *Cratylus*), all public domain.
3. **Risks:** overlap with FI1 (knowledge, Hume, Descartes) handled by linking; formal logic kept light; no false
   balance between science and pseudoscience; respect for religious and non-religious views of reality.
4. **The exam** has an FI4 strand in every sitting (truth, knowledge, science, language, explanation).
5. **Images** within about $0.50: ten nano-banana scenes, 3–5 Mermaid diagrams, and up to five public-domain portraits
   or artworks not used before (Leibniz, Berkeley, Frege, Bacon, or an allegory of truth).
