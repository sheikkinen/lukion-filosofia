## Expected answer, written before the work [M]

1. **Ten topics** (order 21–30): the social contract (Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau); power and legitimacy (Machiavelli,
   Weber paraphrased); freedom (Mill's harm principle, positive and negative freedom); equality and justice (Plato,
   Aristotle, Rawls paraphrased); rights, the rule of law and democracy; liberalism and conservatism; socialism and
   anarchism; nationalism and community (Snellman); utopias and dystopias (More); welfare, the economy and current
   questions (technology, AI, environment, gender and power).
2. **Primary texts** in English (Hobbes, Locke, Mill, Burke, Wollstonecraft), French (Rousseau), Italian
   (Machiavelli), German (Marx and Engels), Latin (More), Greek (Plato, Aristotle), Swedish or Finnish (Snellman),
   Russian or English (Kropotkin), all public domain.
3. **Neutrality is the main risk:** political ideals must be presented symmetrically, each in its own words with its
   strongest critique, with no party politics and no current Finnish politicians named. Political violence (Hobbes'
   state of nature, revolutions, nationalism) gets the sensitive-theme rules where it applies.
4. **The exam** has an FI3 strand in most sittings (social contract, justice, freedom, ideologies, technology).
5. **Images** within about $0.70: ten nano-banana scenes, 3–5 Mermaid diagrams, and up to five public-domain portraits
   or artworks (Hobbes' *Leviathan* frontispiece, Locke, Rousseau, Mill, Wollstonecraft or More's *Utopia* map).
